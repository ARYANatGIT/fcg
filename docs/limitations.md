# Limitations and Operational Guidelines

**ForecastGuard AI — MoES / NCMRWF (Problem Statement ID: 26079)**

---

## 1. Prototype & Regulatory Status
- **Decision-Support Scope:** ForecastGuard AI is a research decision-support prototype engineered to evaluate the feasibility of AI/ML-driven forecast bust detection.
- **Not an Official Warning System:** The outputs generated by ForecastGuard AI (including bust probabilities, confidence percentages, and spatial risk maps) do **not** constitute official weather advisories or severe weather warnings. The India Meteorological Department (IMD) and MoES remain the sole statutory authorities for issuing public weather forecasts, warnings, and bulletins.

---

## 2. Data & Model Limitations

### Synthetic Demonstration Mode vs. Production NWP Ingestion
- Current default deployment runs in `DATA_MODE=synthetic` using high-fidelity demonstration datasets calibrated to Indian subcontinental bounds ($8^\circ\text{N} - 38^\circ\text{N}$, $68^\circ\text{E} - 98^\circ\text{E}$) to validate algorithms without requiring multi-terabyte real-time GRIB downloads.
- Full operational deployment requires connecting authorized NCMRWF NCUM/NEPS archives or NOAA GFS AWS S3 endpoints as architected in `data_pipeline/`.

### Machine Learning & Statistical Boundaries
- **Statistical Association vs. Causality:** SHAP feature attributions describe the mathematical contribution of input variables to the LightGBM decision boundaries. They do **not** provide physical or thermodynamic proof that a specific variable caused a bust.
- **Analog Retrieval vs. Calibrated Probability:** The historical analog retrieval system provides contextual pattern matching ("$3/5$ similar cases experienced busts in the past"). Analog frequency must not be conflated with the calibrated posterior probability produced by the ML model.
- **Run-to-Run Revisions as Risk Signals:** A large forecast revision across consecutive model runs indicates numerical instability between cycles. It is a risk indicator, not deterministic proof of forecast error.

---

## 3. Operational Integration Guidelines for NCMRWF
1. **Model Retraining Cycle:** LightGBM models should be retrained seasonally (pre-monsoon, monsoon, post-monsoon, winter) to capture seasonal regime shifts.
2. **Verification Latency:** Incorporate IMD automatic weather station (AWS) observations alongside ERA5/satellite reanalysis once quality-controlled data becomes available (typically $T+24\text{h}$ to $T+48\text{h}$).
3. **Threshold Tuning:** The 90th percentile threshold for bust labeling should be reviewed by MoES/NCMRWF operational meteorologists for specific severe weather phenomena (tropical cyclones, western disturbances, active/break monsoon spells).
